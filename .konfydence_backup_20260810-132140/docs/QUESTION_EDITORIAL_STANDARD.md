# Konfydence Scenario Editorial Standard

## Product rule

A Konfydence question is a short decision scene, not a cybersecurity quiz. It should be important enough to matter, readable enough to enjoy, and ambiguous enough that a sensible person has to think.

## Non-negotiable structure

Every scored scenario has **exactly three choices**: A, B and C.

- **4 — strongest move:** breaks the scammer/requester's control of the next step and verifies through a channel, process or person the player controls.
- **3 or 2 — plausible middle move:** genuinely cautious or partially safe, but still less independent, less complete or more exposed than the strongest move.
- **0 — risky move:** believable in the moment, not cartoonishly foolish, but preserves the attacker's control or exposes value.

There must be **one and only one** strongest answer.

## What makes a scenario worth keeping

1. **It protects something meaningful.** Money, identity, accounts, safety, reputation, access, privacy or a consequential decision must be at stake.
2. **It could happen to the target player.** Use the channels, language and context that audience actually encounters.
3. **It contains one human pressure cue.** Hurry, authority, connection/familiarity, or a critical action moment should create the tension.
4. **All three choices are readable as human choices.** Never make the wrong option obviously stupid just to manufacture an easy win.
5. **The reveal teaches a reusable rule.** Feedback explains the decision process, not merely “this link was fake.”
6. **It moves quickly.** Enough detail for tension; no unnecessary backstory or lecture.
7. **It has a little life.** Specific details, recognizable moments and restrained wit are welcome. Jokes must never trivialize victim harm or sensitive situations.

## Deck architecture

Each edition contains a **40-scenario bank**:

- 10 Hurry (H)
- 10 Authority (A)
- 10 Connection (C)
- 10 Critical Action (K)

The free readiness check draws **8 scenarios** — 2 per H/A/C/K.

A full run draws **24 scenarios** — 6 per H/A/C/K — and prioritizes unseen cards. Two free checks followed by one full run can therefore expose a player to all 40 bank cards without repetition.

## Repetition test

Two cards are too repetitive when the same surface story and the same decision solve both. Replace the weaker card with a different mechanism, channel, relationship, emotional trigger or consequence.

“Do not click the link” is not a deck strategy. Strong decks test changed payment destinations, compromised real accounts, AI impersonation, QR bait, OAuth permissions, MFA pressure, identity requests, fake checks, task scams, recovery scams, marketplace manipulation, authority impersonation and other distinct decision mechanics.

## Final review test

A card fails if a player can get it right simply by choosing the only answer that does not sound absurd.
